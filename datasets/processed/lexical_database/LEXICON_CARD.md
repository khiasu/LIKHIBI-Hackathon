# Likhibi Nagamese lexicon

Built 14 October 2026 by `python -m nlp_research.lexicon.build_lexicon`.

The lexicon is the word list behind the Likhibi keyboard, and the place where speakers confirm meanings. Every entry records where it came from and how each of its annotations was made. Nothing in it is marked verified until a Nagamese speaker has checked it.

## At a glance

| Measure | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Entries | 6,912 |
| Found in real Nagamese text (New Testament) | 3,029 |
| Curated by people (keyboard list, thematic word lists, place and tribe names) | 3,408 |
| Candidates, waiting for a speaker to accept or reject | 475 |
| Rejected (Hindi function words, web page boilerplate, multi-word lines) | 13 |
| Entries with a part of speech | 2,160 |
| Share of running text whose words have a part of speech | 90.9% |
| Verb forms with a root and suffix analysis | 690 |
| Spelling-variant groups | 243 |
| Entries with candidate English glosses | 1,131 |
| Names with a capitalised display form | 205 |

## Design rule

Only words that real text or a person attests are listed, and every entry says how it is known. No forms are generated by gluing suffixes onto stems, because such lists look large but mostly contain words nobody uses.

| --- | --- | --- |

| Earlier thematic lists (`datasets/raw/vocabulary/`) | Everyday vocabulary with domains (food, family, slang and others) | Collected from references listed in each file's header. Web-scraped and Hindi sections are kept only as candidates. |

No web pages or social media posts were scraped. Everyday words and slang come in through people:
- the keyboard list;
- the review queue below;
- contributions on the Likhibi dashboard.

## How each field is made

| Field | Method | Trust |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `status` | `attested` if the New Testament uses it; `curated` if a person listed it; `candidate` if it only appears in a noisy list | Shown on every entry |
| `pos` | Four methods in order: hand-tagged function words (96 entries); verb paradigms from suffix analysis (a root needs three or more attested endings); English tags projected through IBM Model 1 alignments of 7,578 verse pairs ([Yarowsky and Ngai, 2001](https://aclanthology.org/N01-1026/)); the domain of a curated list | `pos.method` and `pos.confidence` on every entry. Function-word tags (DET, ADP, PRON and others) come only from the hand-tagged list, never from projection. |
| `morphology`, `root`, `features` | Tense, aspect and mood endings (-ise past, -ibo future, -ibole purpose, -ile conditional, -ikena "having done", na- negation), the -khan plural, postpositions written attached (moi + laga) | Rule-based, unverified |
| `orthographic_variants`, `canonical` | Spellings that differ only in sh/s, ee/i, oo/u, v/b or doubled letters, grouped under the most frequent form. No spelling is declared wrong. | Automatic grouping |
| `gloss_candidates` | English words aligned with the Nagamese word in both directions (`auto_glossary.tsv`), plus hand-written glosses for function words | Candidates only. `english_definition` stays empty until a speaker verifies a meaning. |
| `register` | `scripture` (only in the New Testament), `everyday` (only in curated lists) or `both` | Follows the sources |
| `display` | The capitalised form of names, learned from capitals in mid-sentence (Khrista, Kohima). Common words keep lowercase even when the Bible capitalises them (isor, probhu). | Conservative by design |
| `etymology_origin` | English or Hindi loanword from the list a word came from; otherwise Nagamese | Coarse; most entries need a linguist |

Part-of-speech tags follow [Universal Dependencies](https://universaldependencies.org/u/pos/).

## Known limits

- **Two thirds of the entries have no part of speech yet.** They are mostly curated everyday words and loanwords that the parallel verses cannot reach. The tagged third covers 91% of running text.
- **Glosses are not verified.** None is presented as a definition. The aligned verses also give Bible-flavoured meanings, for example "covenant" for vachan.
- **The verb analysis can over-split** a few words that happen to end like verb forms. Every analysis is marked as rule-based.
- **The text is scripture.** Everyday spellings and slang are under-represented until speakers add them through the review queue and the dashboard.

## Verifying entries

`lexicon_review_queue.tsv` lists 871 entries in order of impact: the function words, the 300 most frequent words, then every candidate. A speaker fills in four columns:
- `decision`: keep, reject or variant-of;
- `correct_pos`;
- `correct_gloss`;
- `note`.

The next build reads those decisions, marks the entries verified, and records who verified them.

To rebuild, from the repository root:

```
python -m nlp_research.lexicon.build_lexicon
python -m nlp_research.prediction.build_keyboard_assets
```

The first needs NLTK in the ML environment (`nltk.download("averaged_perceptron_tagger_eng")`). The second rebuilds the keyboard's word tree, its Kneser-Ney tables, the everyday-word prior and the display forms from the lexicon.
